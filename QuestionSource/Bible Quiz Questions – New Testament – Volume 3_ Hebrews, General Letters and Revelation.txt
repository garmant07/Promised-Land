Bible Quiz Questions – New Testament – Volume 3: Hebrews, General Letters and Revelation
Hebrews–Revelation · 56 chapters · 448 questions
Collection index and Volume 1
Each chapter has eight questions: three Easy, three Medium and two Hard. Question order and multiple-choice answer positions are mixed. Hard questions use closer recall, comparison of passages or careful interpretation.
Quotation questions marked WEB follow the World English Bible. Interpretation questions paraphrase the passage; accept a clearly equivalent answer when exact wording is not being tested.
Read each passage in its literary and historical setting. Theological explanations draw on Scripture and the Gospel Worldview course summary. Course-specific views and disputed readings are identified rather than presented as universal agreement. A character’s words are not automatically the narrator’s teaching.
Sources: Gospel Worldview course summary · World English Bible · Old Testament collection
Examples of Bible-edition notes for disputed passages: Mark 16 · John 7:53–8:11 · 1 John 5:6–8

Q5. Multiple choice: How does the opening describe the Son's relation to God's revelation and creation? (1:1-4) Hard
A) God speaks through the Son, who reveals His glory and sustains all things. B) The Son is described only as one ordinary angel among others. C) The Son is presented as unable to reveal anything about God. D) God is said never to have spoken through prophets.

Q2. Multiple choice: How does the opening identify the book's purpose and original setting? (1:1-11) Hard
A) Its sole stated purpose is to provide a date for the end. B) It explicitly addresses only a single modern nation. C) Its original churches are said to be imaginary. D) It is a revelation associated with Jesus Christ, addressed to servants and to actual churches in Asia.
Answer: D. It is a revelation associated with Jesus Christ, addressed to servants and to actual churches in Asia. Its visions must be read as apocalyptic and prophetic communication to real hearers. Christian schools differ about the fulfillment of many images; a modern timetable should not replace the stated setting.
